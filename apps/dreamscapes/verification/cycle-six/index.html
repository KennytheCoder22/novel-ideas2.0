Cycle Five hypothesis, tested again

The earlier route-injected experiment in tools/cycle-five-performance.cjs measured a 12-second desktop improvement from 26.0 to 32.2 FPS. It skipped SVG updates whenever a ride was active, without proving full boarding/return preservation. This cycle measured complete rides afresh, narrowed the skip to a fully opaque ride, and added synchronous restoration and all-style visual checks.

What changed

The external carousel stops repainting only while the seated view is fully opaque. It refreshes synchronously before the first translucent return frame. Mount rendering reuses its shared GPU buffer instead of reallocating it for every differently sized mount, while keeping every original per-mount viewport and pixel dimension.

An initial version retained the large buffer during rides and repeatedly slowed boarding. That version was rejected. The final version keeps the original exact-size buffer during the opaque ride; boarding performance recovered. The intermediate and repeated measurements remain available in the raw evidence.

Preservation and regressions

249 exact image comparisons passed: 180 mount images across 20 mounts, three poses and three styles; nine full seated views; and 60 returned exterior mounts. Camera-to-seat relationships also pass. These frozen-pose checks complement the ordinary-speed recordings below.

58 unit/contract tests, seven observation cases, three selector/lifecycle cases, eight built-output Zora cases and 22 Carnival regression cases passed. Limits and verified bottlenecks

The exterior is still only about 8–10 FPS on this computer, despite improving from roughly 4–5 FPS. Long exterior frames remain. Desktop rides generally reach about 27–32 FPS and phone-sized emulation approaches 58–59 FPS in dream segments. This does not establish physical-phone or Safari performance.

Separate diagnostic traces show main-thread Paint time falling from about 2933ms to 62ms over a 12-second profile. GPU rendering remains about 26ms median and 30ms p95 in the sampled desktop ride. Profiling itself adds overhead; nested trace regions must not be summed. No verified attribution to specific mesh, shader or texture costs was established. A larger external-renderer redesign needs separate visual validation.

Existing model/texture loading already caches assets. No artwork compression, resolution reduction, animation simplification or speculative gameplay changes were applied. Capture overhead is excluded from the performance table.
